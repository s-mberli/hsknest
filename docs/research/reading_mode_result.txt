> Bottom line: Build Reading Mode around **public-domain classical texts + Wikipedia/OER excerpts auto-graded by HSK level**, use **Azure Neural TTS (pre-generated, cached, with word-boundary timing)** for karaoke audio, use **`pinyin-pro` + `Intl.Segmenter` + CC-CEDICT** for the in-browser hover dictionary, and extend the **existing SM-2 deck** with a lightweight "Encountered in reading" vocabulary state (LingQ-style) rather than a parallel SRS. The single biggest implementation lift — and the one that distinguishes a great reader from a mediocre one — is generating and storing **word-level audio alignment data** offline, so playback sync never depends on the browser firing `boundary` events at the right moment.

## 1. Comparable Products — What Makes a Good Reading Experience

### Executive Summary
The Chinese-graded-reader market has consolidated around a handful of mature products. DuChinese is the gold standard for breadth (3,000+ readings, human narration, Pleco integration). HSKStory is the rising competitor with multi-chapter HSK 1-9 fiction and FSRS integration. LingQ defines the *philosophy* of input-based learning (color-coded vocabulary states across all your reading). The Chairman's Bao owns graded news. Readibu is the "bring your own content" reader. - Resist the temptation to gate everything behind subscription on day one — the most-repeated complaint is "free plan too limited" 【turn3search1】.

## 2. Text Sources — Where to Get Chinese Reading Material

### Executive Summary
Legally usable Chinese reading material is abundant but unevenly distributed. Classical literature (四大名著, Tang/Song poetry, 三字经, 山海经) is solidly public domain and available via Project Gutenberg and Wikisource, but is **classical Chinese**, not modern HSK-appropriate prose. The best *modern, graded* free sources are Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), OER textbooks from US universities (CC BY-SA), and the cleaned HSK 3.0 vocabulary list for auto-grading. There is **no single open corpus of modern HSK-graded prose** — you will need to either commission it, generate it under editorial review, or auto-grade Wikipedia/OER excerpts.

### Key Findings

#### A. Public-domain classical texts
- **Project Gutenberg Chinese**: hundreds of titles — 三字经, 山海经, 易经, Tang poetry, Qing/Ming fiction, Chinese classics, Confucian texts (Legge translations) 【turn1search0】【turn1search1】【turn1search4】. License: public domain. **Caveat**: mostly classical Chinese (文言文) and traditional characters; not HSK-appropriate for levels 1-4. Useful for HSK 7-9 advanced readers with annotation.
- **Wikisource (zh.wikisource.org)**: original contributions are CC BY-SA; classical works are public domain; Chinese government/judicial documents are exempt from copyright under Article 5 of Chinese copyright law 【turn1search5】【turn1search6】【turn1search7】. Quality is editor-reviewed. Format: wikitext (parseable).
- **Classical literature (四大名著 etc.)**: all pre-modern works are public domain worldwide. Modern simplified-character editions *are* copyrighted (the simplification/editing is a new work), but the underlying text is free — you can produce your own simplified version with `opencc`/`chinese-conv`.

#### B. Creative Commons / OER
- **Wikipedia Chinese**: CC BY-SA 3.0, ~1.4M+ articles. Variable register — "literary/technical to casual" — but high-quality articles are abundant and ideal for HSK 4-9 【turn0search15】【turn0search17】【turn0search19】. **Caveat**: attribution + share-alike requirement means derivative HSK-tagged versions must stay CC BY-SA.
- **OER Chinese textbooks**: Bellevue College, MSU Asian Studies, University of Texas Austin "Gateway to Chinese" (free interactive dialogues, CC-licensed) 【turn0search10】【turn0search12】【turn0search14】.
- **Free graded reader sites**: chinesegradedreader.com publishes free HSK-level stories 【turn3search13】; Reddit community mentions "Niu Chinese" (free DuChinese-style) and "Hearing Chinese" 【turn2search10】 — license terms need verification before integration.
- **Paul Nation's free graded readers** are English-only 【turn2search10】 — useful as a *template*, not a source.

#### C. Licensed / curated
- Chinese news sites with API/licensing: limited and expensive; TCB's model is bespoke licensing with publishers.
- Graded-reader publishers (BLCUP, Sinolingua, Cheng & Tsui): would license but terms are restrictive and pricing不适合 small-team freemium.
- **Recommendation**: don't pursue licensed publishing at MVP; revisit at scale.

#### D. Community-generated
- User-submitted texts: legally fine if you require a CC BY-SA submission license; quality-control burden is real. LingQ's model (users import their own) sidesteps this entirely.
- Wikipedia excerpts: legally clean under CC BY-SA; widely used by Chinese learners already 【turn0search19】.

#### E. Datasets for difficulty analysis
- **HSK 3.0 vocabulary list**: 11,092 official terms across 9 levels, effective July 2026. Cleaned CSV with pinyin/POS/traditional variants at github.com/ivankra/hsk30 — usable for auto-grading 【turn3search15】【turn3search16】【turn3search17】.
- **Mendeley HSK1-9 dataset**: compiled from HSK Standard Course, sample tests, Developing Chinese — labeled by level for training a classifier 【turn0search17】.
- **BCC corpus frequency data**: used in Pleco forums algorithm for HSK difficulty estimation of arbitrary sentences 【turn0search18】.
- **ACL/CCL benchmark for Chinese complexity-controllable generation** 【turn1search19】.
- Academic work on automatic grading of Chinese text complexity using BERT + syntactic/semantic dependency features 【turn0search15】.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project Gutenberg zh | Public domain | Classical 文言文 | ~hundreds | 7-9 (advanced) | UTF-8 text | High (modernize, simplify, grade) |

### Recommendations
- **MVP content backbone**: (1) hand-curate 20-50 short flagship texts per HSK level (1-6) written by a contracted native editor under work-for-hire (this is the only way to get clean HSK 1-3 prose); (2) auto-grade Wikipedia/OER excerpts for HSK 4-9 fill; (3) classical public-domain texts (simplified) for HSK 7-9 advanced.
- Use the **ivankra/hsk30 CSV** as the auto-grading backbone: tokenize text → look up each word's HSK band → compute coverage histogram → assign level.
- All user-submitted content must be **CC BY-SA** (share-alike) — decide if that's acceptable for the proprietary app, or restrict to PD/CC-BY only.
- **Avoid**: scraping blogs/forums ( murky fair use ), pirated textbook PDFs, machine-translated parallel texts.

## 3. Audio — How to Read Texts Aloud

### Executive Summary
For Chinese, **Microsoft Azure Neural TTS is the clear winner**: best Mandarin neural voices (Xiaoxiao, Yunxi, etc.), native **word-boundary events** with `audioOffset` + `textOffset` (the exact data structure karaoke highlighting needs), full SSML, and ~$9.75–16/1M characters. **Web Speech API** (your current stack) is free and zero-latency but unreliable for Chinese word-boundary events and inconsistent across browsers — keep it as a *fallback* for on-demand single-word pronunciation, not for full-text narration. **OpenAI and Polly are weak for Chinese** per user reports. **ElevenLabs** sounds best but lacks native word-timing data and is expensive. Pre-generate and cache all audio — never synthesize on-demand at read time.

| Provider | Chinese quality | Word-boundary data | SSML | Pricing (/1M chars) | Caching | Verdict for HSK Nest |

**Pre-recorded audio datasets**:
- **Mozilla Common Voice**: CC0, Mandarin included since 2019, 30,000+ hours total 【turn0search6】【turn0search8】. But it's speech *donations* — text/audio pairs are not designed for reading-app use and would need **forced alignment** (e.g., `aeneas`, `whisper` with word timestamps) to recover per-word timing. Useful for an experimental "human voice" tier, not MVP.

### Recommendations
- **Tier 1 (MVP)**: Pre-generate audio with **Azure Neural TTS** (zh-CN-XiaoxiaoNeural + zh-CN-YunxiNeural for male/female alternation). Store the **word-boundary JSON** alongside the MP3 file. This gives you karaoke highlighting "for free" with the highest-quality Chinese TTS available.
- **Tier 2 (fallback)**: Web Speech API for **single-word pronunciation** on the hover popup (your existing flashcard TTS path — reuse it).
- **Tier 3 (premium flagship)**: For ~20-50 curated flagship texts, commission **human narration** (1 narrator, ~$50-150/text via a freelance voice artist) — matches the DuChinese/TCB gold standard and is a marketing differentiator.
- **Cost projection**: 1,000 texts × ~800 chars = 800K chars = **~$8-13 one-time** at Azure. Trivial. Cache as MP3 files in a CDN/static volume; serve from your Docker VPS.
- **Never** synthesize on-demand at read time — latency + cost + inconsistency. Always pre-generate at ingest.

## 4. Hover/Tap Dictionary — How to Define Words on Touch

### Executive Summary
Bundle **CC-CEDICT** (CC BY-SA 3.0, ~1MB compressed) client-side; segment with **`Intl.Segmenter` (native, fast)** as primary + **`pinyin-pro`** for pinyin/polyphonic resolution/tone-sandhi; fall back to a server API only for words outside the bundled HSK 1-6 dictionary subset. The hard problems are segmentation quality and polyphonic-character disambiguation — `pinyin-pro`'s segmentation + custom dictionary handles most cases.

### Key Findings

#### A. Dictionaries
- **CC-CEDICT**: CC BY-SA 3.0, community-maintained, ~120K+ entries, downloadable as plain text 【turn1search10】【turn1search13】【turn1search14】. No native HSK tags but **cross-referenceable** against the HSK 3.0 CSV (ivankra/hsk30) to annotate each entry with HSK level 【turn3search15】.
- **CC-Canto**: alternative/addition, similar license.
- **Pleco dictionary data**: licensed (Oxford, Brill, FLTRP) — no public API; do not integrate 【turn0search6】.
- **MDBG**: online CEDICT-based; has an API but rate-limited — use only for server-side enrichment, not client hover.
- **Hanping**: licensed data; not open.

#### B. Segmentation libraries (JavaScript)
- **`pinyin-pro`** (TypeScript, browser+Node): tone marks, polyphonic handling, tone sandhi for 一/不, surname mode, **HTML ruby output**, built-in segmentation, custom dictionaries, traditional support — the most complete single-package solution 【turn2search0】【turn2search3】.
- **`Intl.Segmenter`** (browser-native, `{ granularity: "word" }`): zero-dependency, fast, decent for Chinese 【turn1search17】. Quality is "good not great" — see comparison study showing jieba-class libraries outperform it on F1 【turn2search5】.
- **`nodejieba` / `@node-rs/jieba`**: native bindings (Rust via N-API), 33% faster than cppjieba, best Node.js performance — server-side only 【turn1search11】【turn1search12】.
- **`jieba-wasm` / `@isdk/nlp-jieba`**: browser-runnable jieba via WASM 【turn1search10】【turn1search13】.
- **Pure-JS `jieba-node`**: exists but less maintained 【turn1search14】.
- Academic comparisons: Ansj/HanLP/Word/Jieba benchmarked — jieba is solid for general text; HanLP higher accuracy but heavier 【turn2search5】.

#### C. Word-identification challenges
- **了, 的/得/地, 成语, 专有名词**: handled by (1) custom dictionary overrides via `pinyin-pro.addDict`, (2) HSK 3.0 word list as a custom lexicon (forces known multi-char words to segment as units), (3) context-dependent meanings addressed by showing *all* CC-CEDICT senses in the popup (Pleco-style) rather than guessing.
- **Out-of-dictionary multi-char words**: fall back to per-character pinyin + show "not in dictionary" with optional server-side lookup.
- **打 (20+ meanings)**: show all senses; let user pick — don't over-engineer automatic disambiguation.

### Recommendations
- **Client-side bundled dictionary**: CC-CEDICT cross-tagged with HSK 3.0 levels, ~1.5MB compressed (gzip/Brotli). Load lazily on first Reading Mode open.
- **Segmentation pipeline**: `Intl.Segmenter("zh-CN", { granularity: "word" })` for fast initial segmentation → `pinyin-pro` for pinyin + polyphonic resolution + ruby HTML output.
- **Hybrid lookup**: bundle HSK 1-6 + CC-CEDICT core (~50K entries, ~1MB); for rare words (HSK 7-9 proper nouns, names), the popup offers a "Look up online" button that hits a server endpoint backed by MDBG or your own expanded dictionary.
- **Ruby rendering**: use HTML `<ruby>` + `<rt>` with CSS `ruby-position: over` (browser support good per Can I Use) 【turn1search19】【turn1search16】.
- **Custom dictionary for idioms & HSK multi-char words**: import the HSK 3.0 word list as a custom lexicon so 成语 and graded words segment as units.

## 5. Integration with SRS — How Reading Connects to Memorization

### Executive Summary
LingQ's model is the reference: every word you encounter gets a **state** (Unknown → Encountered → Learning → Known), color-coded across all texts, and "Learning" words flow into SRS review. The right move for HSK Nest is **not** to build a parallel reading-SRS but to extend the existing SM-2 deck with an **"Encountered in reading" provenance tag** — reading a word updates its SM-2 ease but doesn't create a separate card. Auto-add words the user looks up ≥2 times in a text. Compute per-text "vocabulary coverage %" against the user's known-word set to drive the i+1 difficulty recommendation.

### Key Findings
- **LingQ's state machine**: status 1=New → 2=Recognized → 3=Familiar → 4=Learned; status determines SRS frequency; clicking the green check = "Known" (never due again) 【turn0search14】.
- **DuChinese**: SRS-based flashcards integrated with reading; less granular state tracking than LingQ 【turn0search3】.
- **i+1 / 95-98% threshold**: SLA research (Krashen, Nation, Laufer & Schmitt) strongly indicates learners need **95–98% known-word coverage** for optimal vocabulary acquisition from context; below ~90% comprehension, reading becomes inefficient 【turn2search5】【turn2search6】【turn2search11】【turn2search12】.
- **Extensive reading research (Nation)**: graded readers are *essential* because authentic texts don't repeat vocabulary enough for incidental acquisition; learners should "read easily and fluently, understanding at least 95%" 【turn2search11】【turn2search12】【turn2search14】.
- **FSRS vs SM-2**: FSRS is the modern successor (used by HSKStory, RemNote, open-spaced-repetition ecosystem); SM-2 is your current algorithm — both are fine, FSRS is strictly better but migrating is a separate decision 【turn1search0】【turn1search1】【turn1search2】【turn1search3】.

### Recommendations
- **Vocabulary states (extend existing SM-2 deck)**:
  - `Unknown` — never seen, not in deck
  - `Encountered` — seen in reading, looked up ≥1 time, not yet in deck (lightweight log only, no review schedule)
  - `Learning` — added to SM-2 deck (either from flashcards or auto-added after 2 lookups in 7 days)
  - `Known` — SM-2 ease above threshold, no lookups in last 5 reads
  - `Mastered` — Known + n successful reads without lookup
- **Reading does NOT auto-create SM-2 cards** — it updates the `Encountered` log and may *promote* to `Learning` after a configurable lookup threshold. This avoids polluting the user's deck with every word they tap.
- **Add-to-SRS affordances**: (1) explicit "Add to my vocabulary" button on the popup; (2) auto-promote after N lookups; (3) batch-add all unknown words from a text (one-click before starting a chapter).
- **Difficulty calibration**: compute `coverage%` = (known + mastered words in text) / (total words). Recommend texts at **~85-92% coverage** (i+1 sweet spot — challenging but tractable). Surface this as "This text is 87% within your vocabulary."
- **Adaptive difficulty**: when user finishes a text, suggest next text at slightly higher coverage delta. Track reading history to avoid recommending texts already mastered.

【turn0search14】【turn0search3】【turn2search5】【turn2search6】【turn2search11】【turn2search12】【turn2search14】【turn1search0】【turn1search1】【turn1search2】【turn1search3】

### Open Questions
- Should reading a word count as an SM-2 **review** (resets the interval)? LingQ doesn't; we recommend against (it would inflate ease artificially).
- Do we expose the **"known word count"** as a vanity metric (LingQ-style leaderboard hook)?
- Threshold for auto-promotion: **2 lookups in 7 days**? **3 lookups ever**? Make it user-configurable.

## 6. Visual Design — What Does a Great Reading Interface Look Like

### Executive Summary
Reading-optimized Chinese typography demands **larger fonts, line-height ~1.8-2.0, constrained column width (~20-35 characters/line), and ruby annotations via HTML `<ruby>`**. Use **Noto Sans SC** as the workhorse (with subsetted web fonts to control the 115MB full-file problem) and offer **LXGW WenKai** as a reader-preferred serif/handwriting alternative. Mobile-first single-column; desktop constrained column. Dark mode with warm-tinted background. Karaoke highlighting is the single highest-impact visual feature.

### Key Findings
- **Chinese web fonts are huge**: full Noto Sans CJK SC is ~115MB across 9 weights; per-weight ~16MB — must subset for web delivery 【turn2search19】.
- **Line-height**: Chinese text is denser; recommended **~2.0** vs Latin's 1.5 【turn2search17】.
- **Line length**: Latin ideal is 60-80 chars 【turn2search16】; for Chinese, ~20-35 characters/line is the readability sweet spot (constrained column on desktop).
- **Ruby text**: HTML `<ruby>字<rt>zì</rt></ruby>` with CSS `ruby-position: over|under|inter-character` — well-supported now 【turn1search15】【turn1search16】【turn1search17】【turn1search19】. `pinyin-pro` generates this markup directly 【turn2search0】.
- **Mixed Chinese/English**: use `font-family` fallback chains (Noto Sans SC, then system Latin font) 【turn2search19】.
- **Bopomofo (zhuyin)**: secondary option for Taiwan-focused learners; same ruby mechanism, just different annotation content 【turn1search5】【turn1search8】.
- **Layout patterns**: DuChinese/HSKStory use **constrained-column scrolling text** (not pagination) with a top audio bar and tap-to-popup. Pagination is better for short graded texts; scrolling for long-form.
- **Comprehension aids**: TCB-style keyword/grammar lists at chapter end + comprehension quiz 【turn3search4】.

### Recommendations
- **Typography**: Noto Sans SC (subsetted to ~3,500 common chars + text-specific chars per page, ~200-400KB) as default; offer LXGW WenKai (open-source, reader-friendly serif) as a toggle. Font-size: 18-20px mobile, 20-22px desktop, user-adjustable ±4 steps. Line-height 1.8. Max column width ~680px on desktop.
- **Ruby**: render pinyin above characters via `<ruby>` + CSS; "smart pinyin" mode (only show for words below user's HSK level — HSKStory pattern) 【turn0search19】.
- **Karaoke highlight**: highlight the **current word** during playback (subtle background), with the **current sentence** more prominently underlined. Use Azure's word-boundary offsets to drive this — do not rely on `onboundary`.
- **Mobile**: single column, thumb-reachable audio controls at bottom (thumb zone), font-size control, night mode (warm #1a1a1a bg, #e8d8c0 text).
- **Dark mode**: warm-reduced brightness, not pure black — easier on eyes for long reading.
- **Long texts**: infinite scroll with a thin progress bar at top; bookmark/continue-reading via existing user model.

## 7. Implementation Architecture — How to Build This

### Executive Summary
The architecture centers on a **text-ingestion pipeline** that runs once per text (server-side): segment → annotate pinyin + HSK level → compute difficulty → pre-generate Azure TTS audio + word-boundary JSON → store everything in SQLite. At read time, the client loads the pre-processed text + audio + timing data and does pure playback sync (no live TTS, no live segmentation, no live dictionary API for common words). The hover dictionary is instant because CC-CEDICT is bundled.

### Key Findings — Karaoke Implementation
The reference pattern (Mimi Chen, Medium "Read-Along Player"): **align once, offline** — pre-compute word→audio-offset mapping at ingest, then at runtime the client just listens to `audio.currentTime` and highlights the matching word. No live boundary events, no fuzzy matching, no lag 【turn1search10】【turn1search12】. This is exactly what Azure's `word_boundary` event gives you as a JSON sidecar.

### Recommendations

#### A. Data model (Prisma/SQLite extension)
```
Text: id, title, hskLevel, topic, source, license, author, 
      bodyRaw, bodySegmented (JSON), difficultyScore, 
      estReadingTimeMin, createdAt
TextWord: id, textId, position, surface, lemma, 
         pinyin, hskLevel, cedictEntryId (nullable)
AudioTrack: id, textId, voiceProvider, voiceId, 
            audioUrl, timingsJson (word→offset[]), 
            durationMs, generatedAt
UserReadingProgress: userId, textId, lastWordPosition, 
                    lastReadAt, completedAt
UserVocabState: userId, wordLemma, state 
               (ENCOUNTERED|LEARNING|KNOWN|MASTERED), 
               lookupCount, lastSeenAt, sm2CardId (nullable)
```

#### B. Text-processing pipeline (server, at ingest)
1. **Segment**: `@node-rs/jieba` (Node, fast) → word list.
2. **Pinyin + polyphonic resolution**: `pinyin-pro` server-side.
3. **HSK tagging**: cross-reference each word against HSK 3.0 CSV (ivankra/hsk30).
4. **Dictionary lookup**: match against CC-CEDICT; store `cedictEntryId`.
5. **Difficulty scoring**: % words at each HSK level + BCC corpus frequency for non-HSK words (Pleco-forums algorithm) 【turn0search18】 → assign HSK level.
6. **Audio generation**: call Azure TTS with `wordBoundaryEnabled=true` → store MP3 + timings JSON.
7. **Human review queue**: flag texts with >X% out-of-dictionary words for editor review before publishing.

#### C. Performance
- **Long texts**: load text + timings JSON lazily; render virtualized word spans (`react-virtual` or windowing) for texts >2,000 words.
- **Audio**: pre-loaded MP3 on a static file server (your Docker volume or S3-compatible); stream via `<audio>`. No on-demand synthesis.
- **Hover dictionary**: CC-CEDICT bundled (Brotli-compressed ~1MB); lookup is a `Map.get()` — instant.
- **Offline reading (PWA)**: service worker caches the text + audio + dictionary for already-downloaded texts; allow offline read-aloud.

#### D. Content management
- Admin interface for adding/editing texts (markdown in, processed pipeline out).
- Versioning: store `bodyRaw` revisions; corrections re-run the pipeline.
- Analytics: per-text open rate, completion rate, average lookup rate (signals where texts are too hard).

### Sources
【turn1search10】【turn1search12】【turn0search18】【turn3search15】【turn0search9】

### Open Questions
- **SQLite at scale**: 10K texts × ~500 words × annotations → ~5M rows. SQLite handles this fine for read-heavy workloads, but consider Postgres if you exceed ~50K texts or add full-text search.
- Do we store **audio as files on disk** (Docker volume) or in SQLite BLOBs? Files + CDN is the standard answer.
- **Real-time collaboration** (multiple editors reviewing a text) — not needed at MVP.

## 8. Learning Science — What Makes Reading Effective for Language Acquisition

### Executive Summary
The research is clear: **comprehensible input (Krashen's i+1)** with **95-98% known-word coverage** is the optimal zone for vocabulary acquisition from context. **Extensive reading** (volume at appropriate difficulty) complements SRS — SRS handles *deliberate* encoding, reading provides *incidental* exposure and context-binding. **Bimodal input** (reading + simultaneous listening with karaoke highlighting) aids acquisition. Reading should track **exposures** and feed them as a soft signal to SRS, but not replace explicit review.

### Key Findings
- **Krashen's Input Hypothesis (i+1)**: language is *acquired* (not learned) by processing input slightly beyond current competence; the "+1" is bridged via context 【turn2search6】【turn2search9】.
- **95-98% comprehension threshold** (Nation, Laufer & Schmitt): below ~95% coverage, learners can't infer unknown words from context efficiently; below 90%, reading becomes frustrating and inefficient 【turn2search5】【turn2search11】【turn2search12】.
- **Extensive reading principles (Nation)**: graded readers are necessary because authentic texts don't repeat vocabulary enough; learners should read at ≥95% comprehension for fluency; extensive reading is one tool among several (deliberate study, fluency practice, output) 【turn2search11】【turn2search14】.
- **Vocabulary from context**: research suggests ~5-16 exposures needed to acquire a word incidentally — reading alone is slow for new vocab, which is *why* SRS integration matters 【turn2search13】.
- **Bimodal input (read + listen)**: dual-modality supports comprehension; karaoke-style highlighting helps phonology-orthography binding (research supports, though measured effects vary) 【turn2search8】.
- **Neuro-ecological critique**: pure CI is necessary but not sufficient — interaction, feedback, and multimodal engagement enhance acquisition 【turn2search8】. Implication: Reading Mode should integrate with active SRS, not replace it.

### Recommendations
- **Default difficulty target**: recommend texts at **85-92% known-word coverage** for the active learner (slightly below the 95% fluency threshold — this is the "learning zone" where new vocab is tractable via context + lookup).
- **Two reading modes**:
  - *Fluency mode* (≥95% coverage): for flow-state extensive reading; minimal lookups; tracks exposures only.
  - *Learning mode* (85-94%): i+1 zone; lookups feed SRS auto-promotion.
- **Track exposures**: every time a word appears in a text the user reads to completion, log an `exposure` event. When a word hits N exposures, surface a gentle nudge: "You've seen 这 7 times in reading — add it to your deck?"
- **Karaoke highlighting**: keep on by default; offer "natural speed" and "slow (0.8×)" options. Slower audio helps lower levels; natural speed helps upper levels.
- **Comprehension checks**: end-of-text quiz (3-5 MCQs) for flagship texts only; don't gate every text — gamification fatigue is real.

### Sources
【turn2search6】【turn2search9】【turn2search5】【turn2search11】【turn2search12】【turn2search14】【turn2search13】【turn2search8】

### Open Questions
- Do we surface a **"known word count"** as the primary progress metric (LingQ-style) or hide it?
- What's the right **exposure threshold** before nudging "add to deck"? Start with 5, make configurable.
- Should karaoke highlighting be **word-level** (Azure gives this) or **sentence-level** (simpler, less distracting)? Offer both as a toggle.
